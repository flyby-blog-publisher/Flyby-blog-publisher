<p>Trained as a pilot outside India and now want to fly commercially back home? A foreign CPL or ATPL doesn't automatically let you work in Indian airspace — you'll need to convert it to a valid DGCA licence first. It's a well-defined process, but one with several moving parts. Here's how it actually works.</p>

<h2>Step 1: Confirm Your Base Eligibility</h2>
<p>You need a valid CPL or ATPL issued by an <strong>ICAO-recognised licensing authority</strong>, along with the minimum flight hours required for the licence category. Before applying, gather:</p>
<ul>
<li>Authenticated flight logbooks</li>
<li>A letter of verification from your foreign licensing authority</li>
<li>Educational certificates</li>
<li>Police clearance certificate</li>
</ul>

<h2>Step 2: Clear the DGCA Theoretical Exams</h2>
<p>Even with a valid foreign licence, DGCA requires you to pass three examinations to confirm your knowledge aligns with Indian aviation regulations:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Air Regulations</strong></li>
<li><strong>Composite paper</strong> covering Meteorology and Navigation</li>
<li><strong>RTR(A)</strong> — Radio Telephony Restricted (Aeronautical)</li>
</ul>

<h2>Step 3: Complete Conversion Flying</h2>
<p>You'll need to complete approximately <strong>10–25 hours</strong> of conversion flying at a DGCA-approved flying school. This typically includes day and night general flying, an instrument rating check, and cross-country flight requirements — essentially confirming you can apply your existing skills under Indian airspace procedures and conditions.</p>

<h2>Step 4: Get a DGCA Class 1 Medical Certificate</h2>
<p>Regardless of any medical certification you hold from your foreign licensing authority, you must obtain a fresh <strong>DGCA Class 1 Medical Certificate</strong> from an approved Indian medical examiner. This isn't optional or a formality — budget time for it early in your conversion timeline.</p>

<h2>Step 5: Submit Your Application via eGCA</h2>
<p>Once your exams, conversion flying, and medical are complete, you submit your full documentation and applicable fees (approximately ₹5,000 per endorsement) through DGCA's eGCA portal for final licence issuance.</p>

<h2>How Long Does It Take?</h2>
<p>Most candidates complete the entire conversion process in <strong>3 to 5 months</strong>, assuming documentation is in order and exam attempts go smoothly. Costs typically range from <strong>₹3,00,000 to ₹5,00,000</strong>, covering verification, exam fees, conversion flying hours, and licensing charges.</p>

<h2>Common Delays to Avoid</h2>
<ul>
<li>Incomplete or improperly authenticated logbooks — get these verified early</li>
<li>Underestimating RTR(A) English proficiency requirements</li>
<li>Booking conversion flying hours too late, leading to scheduling backlogs at busy flying schools</li>
</ul>
