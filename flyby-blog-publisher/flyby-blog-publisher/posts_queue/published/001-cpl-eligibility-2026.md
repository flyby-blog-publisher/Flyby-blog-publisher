<p>If you're planning a career as a commercial pilot in India, the first real question isn't "how do I fly" — it's "am I even eligible?" The Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) sets specific requirements for a Commercial Pilot Licence (CPL), and getting familiar with them early saves you time, money, and confusion later. Here's what actually matters.</p>

<h2>Minimum Age</h2>
<p>You must be at least <strong>18 years old</strong> to be issued a CPL, though flight training itself can begin at 17. Many students start ground classes and simulator sessions right after Class 12, so they're flight-test-ready the moment they turn 18.</p>

<h2>Educational Qualification</h2>
<p>A pass in Class 12 with <strong>Physics and Mathematics</strong> is mandatory. If you came from a non-science stream, this isn't a dead end — you can complete Physics and Mathematics separately through NIOS (National Institute of Open Schooling) and still qualify. This is one of the most common questions we get from students who discovered their aviation ambitions late in school.</p>

<h2>Medical Fitness: DGCA Class 1 Medical Certificate</h2>
<p>Before you can apply for or exercise the privileges of a CPL, you need a valid <strong>DGCA Class 1 Medical Certificate</strong>, issued only by a DGCA-approved medical examiner. This is a more rigorous standard than a general fitness check — it covers vision, hearing, cardiovascular health, and more. We strongly recommend getting an initial medical assessment done before you commit to training, since a small number of correctable or borderline conditions are easier to address early.</p>

<h2>Minimum Flying Hours</h2>
<p>A CPL requires a minimum of <strong>200 flight hours</strong>, which must include a mix of:</p>
<ul>
<li>Solo flying time</li>
<li>Cross-country flights</li>
<li>Instrument flying time</li>
<li>Night flying experience</li>
</ul>
<p>These hours are logged progressively through your training and verified before your final licence issuance.</p>

<h2>DGCA Ground Exam Subjects</h2>
<p>Alongside flight training, you'll need to clear five DGCA theory papers, each requiring a minimum of <strong>70% to pass</strong>:</p>
<ul>
<li>Air Navigation</li>
<li>Aviation Meteorology</li>
<li>Air Regulations</li>
<li>Technical General</li>
<li>Technical Specific</li>
</ul>
<p>You'll also need to clear the <strong>RTR(A) — Radio Telephony Restricted (Aeronautical)</strong> exam, which tests your radio communication procedures and English language proficiency to ICAO Level 4 or higher.</p>

<h2>Putting It All Together</h2>
<p>None of these requirements are meant to filter people out arbitrarily — they exist because a commercial pilot is responsible for passenger lives from day one. The good news is that with the right guidance, a structured study plan, and consistent flight training, most motivated students clear these milestones within 18–24 months.</p>
