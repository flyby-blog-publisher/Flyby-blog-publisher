<p>For many students, the decision to become a pilot forms early — sometimes as early as school. But the path from "I want to fly" to "I'm a commercial pilot" involves several concrete, sequential steps. Here's the complete roadmap, laid out simply.</p>

<h2>Step 1: Choose the Right Stream in Class 11–12</h2>
<p>You'll need Physics and Mathematics in Class 12 to be eligible for a Commercial Pilot Licence (CPL). If you're still in Class 10 or 11 and know you want to fly, choosing Science with PCM keeps every door open. If you've already passed 12th in a different stream, don't worry — Physics and Maths can be completed separately through NIOS.</p>

<h2>Step 2: Get a Preliminary Medical Assessment</h2>
<p>Before investing in ground classes, get an early medical check against DGCA Class 1 Medical standards. This is one of the most overlooked steps — and one of the most important, since certain vision or health conditions are easier to address (or rule out early) before you've spent money on training.</p>

<h2>Step 3: Choose a Flying School and Start Ground Classes</h2>
<p>Ground classes cover the five DGCA theory subjects — Air Navigation, Aviation Meteorology, Air Regulations, Technical General, and Technical Specific — plus RTR(A) exam preparation. This phase typically overlaps with the early part of your flight training and takes several months of consistent study.</p>

<h2>Step 4: Complete Flight Training (200 Hours Minimum)</h2>
<p>This is the core of your CPL journey — solo flying, cross-country flights, instrument flying, and night flying, building toward the 200-hour minimum required for licence issuance. Expect this phase, combined with ground school, to take roughly 18–24 months in total.</p>

<h2>Step 5: Clear DGCA Exams and Apply for Your CPL</h2>
<p>Once you've cleared all five theory papers (minimum 70% each), passed RTR(A), and logged your required hours, you apply for your CPL through DGCA's eGCA portal.</p>

<h2>Step 6: Build Additional Qualifications</h2>
<p>Most airlines look for more than a bare CPL. Depending on your target airline and aircraft, you may pursue:</p>
<ul>
<li>An <strong>Aircraft Type Rating</strong> (e.g. A320, B737)</li>
<li>A <strong>Multi-Crew Coordination (MCC)</strong> course</li>
<li>Additional hours-building through instructing or smaller operators, if required by your target employer</li>
</ul>

<h2>Step 7: Apply for Airline First Officer Roles</h2>
<p>With your CPL, type rating, and MCC in hand, you're eligible to apply for First Officer positions. From there, your career progresses through accumulated flying hours toward an eventual ATPL upgrade and Captain-level seniority.</p>

<h2>A Realistic Timeline</h2>
<p>Most students move from "just finished 12th" to "licensed commercial pilot" in about <strong>2 to 2.5 years</strong>, assuming steady progress without major delays. Budget-wise, plan for a total investment in the range of ₹15,00,000–₹25,00,000 through CPL, with type rating and MCC as additional costs closer to your job search.</p>
