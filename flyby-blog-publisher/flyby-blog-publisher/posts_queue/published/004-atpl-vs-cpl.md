<p>If you're new to aviation career research, the acronyms alone can be overwhelming. Two of the most commonly confused terms are CPL and ATPL. Here's the plain-language difference — and why you don't actually get to choose between them the way it might sound.</p>

<h2>CPL: Where Every Commercial Pilot Starts</h2>
<p>A <strong>Commercial Pilot Licence (CPL)</strong> is the entry-level professional pilot licence. It's what allows you to be paid to fly, and it's the licence every aspiring airline pilot earns first. To qualify, you need to be at least 18, hold Class 12 with Physics and Maths, pass a DGCA Class 1 medical, log a minimum of 200 flying hours, and clear five DGCA theory papers plus the RTR(A) exam.</p>
<p>With a CPL, you can fly as a co-pilot / first officer for an airline, or take up flying jobs like charter operations, aerial survey work, or flight instruction (with additional certification).</p>

<h2>ATPL: The Airline Captain's Licence</h2>
<p>An <strong>Airline Transport Pilot Licence (ATPL)</strong> is the highest level of pilot certification, and it's what you need to serve as a <strong>Pilot-in-Command (Captain)</strong> on a commercial airline. Unlike CPL, you can't start your training aiming directly for an ATPL — it's earned by upgrading your CPL after you've accumulated significantly more flying hours (well beyond the 200-hour CPL minimum) and cleared additional theory and flight-test requirements.</p>

<h2>So Which One Should You Pursue First?</h2>
<p>This isn't really an either/or decision — it's sequential:</p>
<ol>
<li>You train for and earn your <strong>CPL</strong></li>
<li>You get hired (typically as a First Officer) and accumulate flying hours over several years</li>
<li>Once eligible, you upgrade to an <strong>ATPL</strong>, which qualifies you for Captain-level roles</li>
</ol>
<p>Every airline captain in the country started with a CPL. The real planning question for a new student isn't "CPL or ATPL" — it's making sure your CPL training, flying school reputation, and initial hours-building strategy set you up well for the ATPL upgrade down the line.</p>

<h2>Cost Considerations</h2>
<p>CPL training typically costs in the range of ₹15,00,000–₹25,00,000 including flying hours. The ATPL upgrade, pursued later once you're already earning as a First Officer, adds a further ₹5,00,000–₹7,00,000 — usually a far more manageable expense at that career stage, and sometimes partly supported by your employer airline.</p>
